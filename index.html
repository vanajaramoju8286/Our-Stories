YEARS OF THE SAME POUR

Served in brass, as it was.

A solid heavy-gauge brass tumbler resting in the dabarah bowl, radiating warmth before the morning pour.

First, the slow lift.

The tumbler ascends, separating the vessel from the saucer to prepare for the high-altitude meter aeration.

Then the metre pour.

One unbroken ribbon from tumbler to dabara. It cools the cup, marries milk and decoction, and wakes the aroma.

Froth, and golden stillness.

The stream halts, leaving a rich velvety golden-caramel foam resting atop the brass vessel ready for the first sip.

THE SACRED 80:20 RATIO

Slow gravity drip,
perfected in brass.

True South Indian filter coffee is an exacting craft. Eighty percent high-grown shade Arabica roasted slow to develop rich cocoa notes, married with twenty percent French chicory for velvety body, caramel viscosity, and lasting golden froth.

01

12-Hour Gravity Decoction

Water drips drop-by-drop through micro-perforated brass, yielding a thick, concentrated first extraction without astringency.

02

Scalded Milk at 85°C

Rich full-fat milk warmed just beneath boiling to unlock caramelized natural sweetness without scorching.

03

The Metre Aeration

Poured from an elevation between tumbler and dabara to form an airy, luscious froth blanket on top.
